Functional architecture

Nine verticals, sequenced — not designed as if they ship together. Status is against the working system, not the roadmap.

The organising principle, in the founder's words. A doctor should be looking at the patient, not the screen; a retailer should be looking at the customer, not the gadget. Every module below is judged by whether it takes attention away from the counter. That is why voice is the interaction model rather than a feature, and why the analytics layer is not a dashboard — a shop owner who has neither the time nor the training to read one currently outsources understanding of their own business to an auditor once a quarter. Being able to ask the shop a question, in their own language, is the same idea as billing by voice: remove the interface between the owner and their own trade.

What the status table actually says

Of nine verticals, one is complete (voice billing), three are partial, five are untouched. That is the correct shape for a pre-seed prototype and the deck should present it as deliberate sequencing rather than let it read as thin coverage. The sequencing logic is: anything that does not serve a single counter on day one waits.

Two decisions worth defending

Analytics moves to Tier 1. Conventional sequencing puts dashboards at Tier 2+. The founder's point inverts it: the owner who most needs to understand their own numbers is precisely the one who cannot read a dashboard and cannot afford a consultant. A natural-language question — "how much did I sell of coffee powder last week", asked aloud in Tamil — costs almost nothing to build on top of a schema this small, and is a sharper differentiator than any feature Petpooja has. It is also the only module where an LLM clearly earns its place (see the technical note).

Order management is deferred, not scheduled. Modifiers, KDS and aggregator integration are where Petpooja has a decade of depth. Building toward them means competing on their ground with a tenth of the resources. The beachhead is provision and kirana counters, where order management is one line and a total.

Where RAGaaS attaches

VBS knows what was sold and to whom. RAGaaS knows the conversation with that customer. The join is the mobile number already captured at the counter — no new identity system, no new data model. That makes module 5 an integration rather than a build, and it is the only reason a solo founder can credibly show a CRM story at all.
