Bolo Bill — voice-first billing for Indian retail

Pre-seed · ₹60 lakh · Chennai · Karthik Srinivasan, solo founder · bolo-bill.vercel.app — the product is live and you can try it

A doctor should be looking at the patient, not the screen. A retailer should be looking at the customer, not the gadget. Every billing system built for Indian retail asks the shopkeeper to look away — find an item, type a quantity, fix a typo — while a customer waits. So the shops that can least afford it keep the notebook, and 88% of Indian retail remains unorganised trade.

Bolo Bill removes the interface. The shopkeeper speaks — in Tamil, Hindi, Malayalam, Telugu, Kannada or English, code-mixed the way people actually talk — and the bill assembles line by line, ending in a UPI QR for the exact amount and a numbered GST-compliant receipt. It runs on the phone they already own plus a ₹2,500 printer, against the ₹9,990–37,000 billing machines this market is otherwise sold.

What exists — a working product, not a mockup

Live and in use at Ragas Coffee, a working café: ~50 bills over one week, and within that week the operator moved from press-to-talk to the hands-free wake word. Six languages, multi-item dictation in one breath, per-shop spoken aliases, confidence gating, gapless receipt numbering, WhatsApp delivery. 1,561 automated tests, including a 1,003-case matrix across five languages. Built solo, on no outside capital.

The insight underneath the speed

The café's owner does not know objectively what margin he makes. He is not unusual — owners have neither the time nor the training to read a dashboard, so understanding of their own business is outsourced to an auditor once a quarter. So the same idea that removes typing from billing also removes the spreadsheet from knowing your numbers: ask the shop a question aloud, get an answer aloud. That is a Tier-1 module here, not a Tier-2 dashboard, and it is the clearest unmet need we have observed first-hand.

Business model — priced against what the shop already pays

₹399/month for a single counter. Ragas pays WeP ₹583/month in annual maintenance today, on top of sunk capex, for a machine that cannot tell him his margin — that is the reference price, not a free tier. ₹1,999 for 2–10 outlets, undercutting Petpooja's reported ₹3,000–12,000 band. Hardware kit sold at cost, never at margin: we do not manufacture, hold stock, or touch payment funds.

The number this business turns on, stated plainly

On cloud ASR a typical shop is −₹358/month gross. Moving inference on-device takes the same shop to +₹362 at 91% margin — LTV ₹9,050, CAC ₹800, 11.3× LTV:CAC, payback 2.2 months. Break-even on cloud is ₹0.0603 per utterance; above that, Tier 1 cannot be profitable at any volume. That is what this round is for — moving inference on-device, not adding features.

Why it is defensible after ASR commoditises

It will, and that is fine — voice recognition is not the moat. The moat is the per-shop alias catalog (each shop teaches the system its own words; after a month it knows this shop's "aloo" is that SKU — switching cost the shop built themselves) and the corrected-utterance corpus, which is the only realistic route to an on-device model tuned to code-mixed retail speech. The moat and the cost structure are the same asset.

What is not proven — said here rather than found later

  • Offline billing does not exist yet. Against Vyapar that is a disqualification, not a gap. It is the first thing this round buys.
  • Nobody but the founder has spoken to the system. Accent and noise testing used synthetic voices.
  • Retention is unknown. One week at one shop. The 4%/month churn in the model is its weakest number.
  • The on-device ASR target is unvalidated. If a small quantised model cannot hit accuracy on code-mixed retail speech, pricing has to move. It is spiked in months 1–3, before headcount.

The ask

₹60 lakh pre-seed, backed out of an 18-month plan rather than rounded: ₹36L engineering (one ASR/Android engineer — the on-device port is the round), ₹12L field and onboarding, ₹8L infrastructure and devices, ₹4L buffer. Founder unpaid in Year 1. It buys four things: offline billing, inference on-device and under break-even, ten retained shops, and measured error and churn — which is what turns a working prototype into a fundable business.
