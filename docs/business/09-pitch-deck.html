Bolo Bill

The billing app for the shop that still bills on paper.

Karthik Srinivasan · solo founder · working product, one live shop

1
The problem

A doctor should look at the patient.
A retailer should look at the customer.

Every billing system built for Indian retail asks the shopkeeper to look away — to find an item in a list, type a quantity, correct a typo — while a customer waits and three more queue behind.

So the shop that can least afford it keeps the notebook. 88% of Indian retail is still unorganised trade.

And it goes deeper than speed

The owner of our design partner — a working café — does not know objectively what margin he makes.

He is not unusual. Owners have neither the time nor the training to read a dashboard, so understanding of their own business is outsourced to an auditor once a quarter.

The gadget is between the retailer and the customer. The spreadsheet is between the owner and their own trade.

2
The insight

Voice is not a feature here. It is the removal of the interface.

Nobody types

Not to bill. Not to add a product. Not to set a price. Typing is the fallback, never the default — which removes the literacy and typing-speed gate on who can staff a counter.

Nobody reads a dashboard

Ask the shop a question aloud, in your own language, and it answers. The same idea as billing by voice, applied to knowing your own numbers.

Nobody buys a terminal

The phone in their hand plus a ₹2,500 printer. The trial decision shrinks from a week's thought to a quiet hour.

The bar we hold ourselves to: faster than paper, or it does not ship. Not faster than a POS — faster than the notebook it actually replaces.

6
Defensibility

ASR will commoditise. That is fine — it is not the moat.

What is not a moat

Voice recognition. Sarvam is a vendor; Bhashini and open on-device models are moving fast. Whatever we buy today is free tomorrow — for us and for Vyapar, who have the offline engine, the languages and the distribution already.

We plan against Vyapar shipping voice, not past it.

What is

1 · The per-shop alias catalog. Each shop teaches the system its own words for its own goods. After a month it knows that this shop's "aloo" is that SKU at that price. Switching cost the shop built themselves, invisible to a competitor quoting less.

2 · The corrected-utterance corpus. The only realistic route to an on-device model tuned to code-mixed retail speech — and therefore to ₹0 marginal inference.

These are the same asset. The moat and the cost structure are one thing.

Business model

Priced against what the shop already pays — not against free

Single · ₹399/mo

One counter. Voice billing, GST receipt, ask-your-shop analytics.

Ragas pays WeP ₹583/mo in AMC today, on top of sunk capex, for a machine that cannot tell him his margin. That is the reference price — not Vyapar's free tier.

Growing · ₹1,999/mo

2–10 outlets. Multi-counter, order management, CRM via RAGaaS, loyalty. Undercuts Petpooja's ₹3,000–12,000 band and includes what they bill as add-ons.

Chain · ₹7,500/mo

10+. HQ reporting, predictive replenishment, anomaly detection. No product yet — Year 3

Hardware kit sold at cost (~₹4,000), never at margin. It is an adoption lever and a support-cost control. The moment it earns margin we are carrying inventory, which is the thing this strategy exists to avoid.

Unit economics

The one number this business turns on

Today — cloud ASR

Typical shop, 1,500 bills/mo ≈ 6,000 utterances

−₹358

gross profit per shop per month. Negative. The ₹10/shop cost ceiling in our own design doc was right.

After the port — on-device

Same shop, same price, zero marginal inference

+₹362

91% gross margin. LTV ₹9,050 · CAC ₹800 · 11.3× LTV:CAC · payback 2.2 months.

Break-even cloud rate is ₹0.0603 per utterance. Above it, Tier 1 cannot be profitable on cloud at any volume. We state it this way so the case does not rest on a vendor quote we have not yet validated — this is what the round is for: moving inference on-device. Not features.

The caveat we lead with: at low volume the labour saving is small. A café does not buy this to save time — it buys it to stop paying the AMC, to avoid the next machine, and because the owner does not know his margins. Time savings only dominate at kirana volume, which is one more reason the beachhead is provision and kirana rather than cafés, despite our pilot being a café.

Why now, and why me

Timing and founder-market fit

Why now

  • Voice UPI has normalised speaking to a payment. The behaviour is no longer novel.
  • Indic ASR crossed usable for code-mixed speech in the last 24 months.
  • Quick commerce has pushed 84% of kirana into adopting something — defensively.
  • 2025–26 Indian funding rewards unit economics over blitzscale. This plan is built that way rather than apologising for it.

Why me

  • Built the whole product solo — six languages, tested grammar, live receipts, real shop.
  • Also building RAGaaS, which supplies the CRM layer. The integration is not speculative.
  • Chennai-based, in the market the product is designed for, in the language it is designed for.
  • Found and fixed three silent mispricing bugs before any customer met them. That discipline is the product.

Faster than paper, or it does not ship.
